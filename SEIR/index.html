SEIR solver

This webpage uses the Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg fourth-order method with fifth-order error checking RKF45 to approximate the solution to the SEIR equations with the δ\deltaδ parameter to account for quarantine effects:

dSdt=ΛN−μS−βI(1−δ)SNdEdt=βI(1−δ)SN−(μ+a)EdIdt=aE−(γ+μ)IdRdt=γI−μR.\begin{aligned} \frac{dS}{dt} & = \Lambda N - \mu S - \frac{\beta I (1-\delta)S}{N} \\ \frac{dE}{dt} & = \frac{\beta I (1-\delta) S}{N} - (\mu +a ) E \\ \frac{dI}{dt} & = a E - (\gamma +\mu ) I \\ \frac{dR}{dt} & = \gamma I - \mu R. \end{aligned}dtdS​dtdE​dtdI​dtdR​​=ΛN−μS−NβI(1−δ)S​=NβI(1−δ)S​−(μ+a)E=aE−(γ+μ)I=γI−μR.​

Where SSS is the number of susceptible persons, EEE is the number of exposed persons, III is the number of infectious persons and RRR is the number of recovered persons. aaa is the inverse of the average incubation period. β\betaβ is a parameter that pertains to the average number of contacts per person per time and the rate of transmission for the disease. γ\gammaγ is the inverse of the average time a person is infected with the disease. Λ\LambdaΛ is the birth rate. μ\muμ is the overall population death rate (not only including the disease death rate). NNN is the total population.

My original model had γI\gamma IγI multiplied by 1−δ1-\delta1−δ, but as quarantine should not affect how long it takes for people to recover, it should not affect this term.
