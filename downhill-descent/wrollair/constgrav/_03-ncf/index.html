Non-conservative forces

It is important to note, however, that nonconservative forces — such as air resistance and rolling resistance — are a major factor in determining the speed of a tricycle going downhill. To account for these effects, we need to calculate generalized forces. Fortunately, since our problem only has a single independent variable — that of distance from the summit — calculating the generalized forces should be straightforward.

Rolling resistance is proportional to the normal force. The normal force could be calculated as:

N=mgcos⁡θ(s).\begin{aligned} N=mg\cos{\theta(s)}. \end{aligned}N=mgcosθ(s).​

The rolling resistance force is defined as:

Fr=CrrN.\begin{aligned} F_r &= C_{rr}N. \end{aligned}Fr​​=Crr​N.​

Where CrrC_{rr}Crr​ is the coefficient of rolling resistance. Substituting Equation (1) into Equation (1) yields:

Fr=mgCrrcos⁡θ(s).\begin{aligned} F_r &= mgC_{rr}\cos{\theta(s)}. \end{aligned}Fr​​=mgCrr​cosθ(s).​

This force directly opposes motion in the direction of sss.

As for air resistance (or drag):

FD=ρCDAv22.\begin{aligned} F_D &= \dfrac{\rho C_D A v^2}{2}. \end{aligned}FD​​=2ρCD​Av2​.​

Substituting Equation (??) into Equation (3) yields:

FD=ρCDAs˙22.\begin{aligned} F_D &= \dfrac{\rho C_D A \dot{s}^2}{2}. \end{aligned}FD​​=2ρCD​As˙2​.​

Where ρ\rhoρ is air density in kg⋅m−3\mathrm{kg} \cdot \mathrm{m}^{-3}kg⋅m−3, CDC_DCD​ is a dimensionless drag coefficient, AAA is the cross sectional surface area (in m2m^2m2) that the area acts on. This is also in the opposite direction to motion. Hence the generalized non-conservative forces acting on our tricycle would be:

Qs=−mgCrrcos⁡θ(s)−ρCDAs˙22.\begin{aligned} Q_s &= -mgC_{rr} \cos{\theta(s)} - \dfrac{\rho C_D A\dot{s}^2}{2}. \end{aligned}Qs​​=−mgCrr​cosθ(s)−2ρCD​As˙2​.​
