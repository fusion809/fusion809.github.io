Euler-Lagrange equation with non-conservative forces

The Euler-Lagrange equation with dissipative forces in the sss coordinate is:

ddt∂L∂s˙−∂L∂s=Qs.\begin{align} \dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{s}} - \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial s} &= Q_s. \tag{1} \end{align}dtd​∂s˙∂L​−∂s∂L​​=Qs​.​(1)​

The left-hand side of Equation (1) can also be denoted as δSδs\dfrac{\delta S}{\delta s}δsδS​, the functional derivative of SSS with respect to sss. SSS is the action and is defined by:

S=∫t0tfL(s,s˙,t)dt\begin{aligned} S=\int_{t_0}^{t_f} L(s, \dot{s}, t) dt \end{aligned}S=∫t0​tf​​L(s,s˙,t)dt​

where t0t_0t0​ is the starting time and tft_ftf​ is the finishing time. First, we shall calculate ∂L∂s˙\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{s}}∂s˙∂L​, the canonical momentum:

∂L∂s˙=ms˙.\begin{aligned} \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{s}} &= m\dot{s}. \end{aligned}∂s˙∂L​​=ms˙.​

Hence:

ddt∂L∂s˙=ms¨.\begin{align} \dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{s}} &= m\ddot{s}. \tag{2} \end{align}dtd​∂s˙∂L​​=ms¨.​(2)​

As for the second term in Equation (1), it is:

∂L∂s=mgsin⁡θ(s).\begin{aligned} \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial s} &= mg\sin{\theta(s)}. \end{aligned}∂s∂L​​=mgsinθ(s).​

So the functional derivative is therefore:

δSδs=ms¨−mgsin⁡θ(s).\begin{aligned} \dfrac{\delta S}{\delta s} &= m\ddot{s} - mg\sin{\theta(s)}. \end{aligned}δsδS​​=ms¨−mgsinθ(s).​

ms¨=mg(sin⁡θ(s)+Crrcos⁡θ(s))−ρCDAs˙22.\begin{aligned} m\ddot{s} &= mg(\sin{\theta(s)}+C_{rr}\cos{\theta(s)})-\dfrac{\rho C_D A\dot{s}^2}{2}. \end{aligned}ms¨​=mg(sinθ(s)+Crr​cosθ(s))−2ρCD​As˙2​.​
