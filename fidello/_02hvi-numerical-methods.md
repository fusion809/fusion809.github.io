### Numerical solution techniques
In this article, however, we will encounter differential equations that have no exact solutions and for which numerical solutions are necessary. Numerical solution methods approximate the solution to a differential equations, and as such they are not exact. Although, quite often we can make them as accurate as we need them to be (even greater than ten significant figures).

The first three methods we will cover &mdash; Euler's method, Runge-Kutta methods and Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg 4/5th order method &mdash; all are most applicable to initial value problems. After this, we cover the finite difference method and spectral and related methods which are most easily applicable to linear differential equations (although, they can be adapted to work with nonlinear differential equations, too). A major advantage they have is that they can work with any conditions we may wish to impose on our differential equation's solution. After these sections, we briefly cover how partial differential equations can be numerically integrated.
