# Connections to other branches of physics
Classical mechanics provides the foundation that other fields of physics build on. 

For instance, special relativity provides corrections (often called relativistic corrections) to classical mechanics to account for effects that occur at speeds comparable to $c$, the speed of light in a vacuum. General relativity accounts for these effects, too, but also accounts for the effects of strong gravitational fields. 

Quantum mechanics instead focuses on what happens on small scales, namely the scale of atoms and subatomic particles. Like classical mechanics, quantum mechanics does not account for effects that occur at speeds comparable to $c$. 

Quantum field theory focuses on quantum fields, such as those pertaining to electromagnetism, the strong nuclear force and the weak nuclear force. It also has the distinction of being relativistic &mdash; that is, it accounts for effects that occur at speeds comparable to $c$.
